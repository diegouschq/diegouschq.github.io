Is Owning an Airbnb in San Diego a good investment?

Background

Airbnb provides comprehensive datasets that allow us to analyze the market landscape in San Diego, California. We will leverage one of these datasets to determine whether investing in an Airbnb property represents a viable and lucrative business opportunity. To evaluate this, our analysis will focus on three core business questions:

  • What is the average annualized revenue by property type?
  • Which submarkets and regions yield the highest financial returns?
  • How do guest reviews and ratings correlate with listing income?

To contextualize our findings, it is helpful to first define the primary room types available on the platform:

  • Entire Home/Apartment: Guests have the whole place to themselves. This typically includes a private entrance, bedroom, bathroom, and kitchen, with no shared living spaces or host present during the stay.

  • Private Room: Guests have their own private bedroom to sleep in, but other areas of the property (such as the kitchen, living room, or bathroom) are shared with the host or other guests.

  • Shared Room: Guests sleep in a space that is shared with others. This could mean sharing a bedroom with multiple beds (like a hostel style setup) or sleeping in a common area like a living room futon or sectioned-off space.

Room Type - Influence in the market

When entering a short-term rental market, one of the first and most critical decisions is choosing the right property type. Does a higher volume of cheaper private rooms outperform a few high-value entire homes? Below, we explore the relationship between room types, their price distributions, and their ultimate earning potential in San Diego.

Looking thought the average price data between room types, we can see that there is a clear pricing hierarchy across property types, with entire homes commanding the highest rates, followed by private and shared rooms. Analysis of the average price data reveals a distinct pricing hierarchy across property types, following the pattern: Shared Room < Private Room < Entire Apartment/Home. Each tier roughly doubles in price (a +200% increase) compared to the previous one.

While this data highlights clear revenue potential, price distributions in the entire home/apartment and private room categories show wide variations. Constant market fluctuations make these higher-tier properties riskier to invest in. In contrast, shared rooms exhibit the most stable pricing within their category, offering a lower-risk profile.

Looking through all these facts, there are two ways to make a good investment:

  • To earn a great revenue, the best investment would be to rent an entire apartment or Private room (Figure 4). These ones also come with higher market risk.

  • To make some extra money, you could rent out only a portion of your property. Since the demand for shared rooms is higher, there is greater stability and predictability in receiving income (Table 1).

From the whole analysis, these are some of the main insights:

  • Location plays a significant role in revenue. For the most part, properties near the coast generate better revenue for landlords compared to those in the center of the city.
  • Higher revenue properties do not commonly face high local competition, as areas with top revenue tend to have a smaller supply of rentals in the neighborhood.
  • Potential investors and renders need to analyze which scenario would be most beneficial to them, depending on their investment amount and property holdings.

WITH aggregated_listings AS (
    SELECT 
        neighbourhood_cleansed AS loc_neighborhood,
        COUNT(id) AS num_units,
        CAST(AVG(TRY_CAST(REPLACE(REPLACE(price, '$', ''), ',', '') AS DECIMAL(10,2))) AS DECIMAL(10,2)) AS avg_price,
        AVG(365 - availability_365) AS avg_occupancy,
        CAST(AVG(accommodates) AS DECIMAL(10,0)) AS accommodates,
        CAST(AVG(review_scores_accuracy) AS DECIMAL(10,2)) AS r_accuracy,
        CAST(AVG(review_scores_cleanliness) AS DECIMAL(10,2)) AS r_clean,
        CAST(AVG(review_scores_communication) AS DECIMAL(10,2)) AS r_communication,
        CAST(AVG(review_scores_location) AS DECIMAL(10,2)) AS r_location,
        CAST((CAST(AVG(reviews_per_month) AS DECIMAL(10,2)) * 12) AS DECIMAL(10,2)) AS Yreview_interactions,
    FROM listings 
    WHERE price IS NOT NULL
        AND TRIM(price) != '' -- Filter out empty strings
        AND availability_365 IS NOT NULL
        AND room_type IS NOT NULL 
        -- Safe numeric comparison with TRY_CAST:
        AND TRY_CAST(REPLACE(REPLACE(price, '$', ''), ',', '') AS DECIMAL(10,2)) <= 5000
    GROUP BY neighbourhood_cleansed
)

SELECT 
    loc_neighborhood,
    num_units,
    (avg_price * avg_occupancy) AS total_revenue,
    accommodates,
    r_accuracy,
    r_clean,
    r_communication,
    r_location,
    Yreview_interactions,
CASE
    -- Large established markets
    WHEN num_units >= 100
         AND total_revenue >= 40000
         AND r_location >= 4.85
         AND Yreview_interactions >= 15
    THEN 'Established High-Performing Market'

    -- Small sample but exceptional revenue
    WHEN num_units < 30
         AND total_revenue >= 60000
         AND r_location >= 4.85
    THEN 'Luxury / High Potential'

    -- Solid investment opportunity
    WHEN total_revenue >= 35000
         AND avg_occupancy >= 200
         AND r_location >= 4.80
    THEN 'Growing Opportunity'

    -- High ratings but limited demand
    WHEN r_location >= 4.90
         AND Yreview_interactions < 10
    THEN 'Quality Market - Low Demand'

        ELSE 'Moderate Market'
    END AS market_category
FROM aggregated_listings
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC;

Just looking at this table is possible to deduct some ideas of what is the customer preference based on the top 10 highest revenue of properties:

  • Guest satisfaction is consistently high across neighborhoods, with most ratings above 4.8, suggesting that differences in revenue are driven more by location and market demand than by service quality.

Summary Points

The findings indicate that successful investments depend on balancing expected revenue, market competition, and investment risk:

  • Among all property types, entire homes/apartments generate the highest average annual revenue, making them the most attractive option for investors seeking maximum returns.

  • Private rooms provide a moderate balance between revenue and affordability.

  • shared rooms generate the lowest income but achieve the highest occupancy rates, offering a more stable and predictable source of revenue.

  • Higher-priced property types also experience greater price variability, resulting in increased investment risk.

  • Location is one of the strongest drivers of financial performance. Properties located near to the coast consistently outperform those farther inland.

  • Many high-performing neighborhoods combine strong demand with relatively limited competition, suggesting that high market demand can reduce revenue potential.

  • Service quality alone does not explain differences in revenue. Customer ratings remain consistently high across nearly all neighborhoods, with average review scores exceeding 4.8 out of 5.
